XXXTENTACION: THE SHORT LIFE OF JAHSEH ONFROY
English narration script · approx. 10 minutes (~1450 words)

[00] COLD OPEN
June 18, 2018. Deerfield Beach, Florida. A black BMW pulls out of a motorcycle dealership. Minutes later, the most streamed new artist in America is gone. He was twenty years old. Three months earlier, his album had debuted at number one. One week later, his song "SAD!" would top the Billboard Hot 100. His name was Jahseh Onfroy. The world knew him as XXXTentacion. This is the story of a life that was loud, brilliant, troubled, and far too short.

[01] PLANTATION, FLORIDA
Jahseh Dwayne Ricardo Onfroy was born on January 23, 1998, in Plantation, Florida, to parents of Jamaican descent. His childhood was unstable. He was raised for long stretches by his grandmother, Collette Jones, and he moved between homes and schools. Music came early. His grandmother took him to church, where he sang in the choir. He learned guitar and piano. He later said he often felt angry and alone as a child, and that music was the one place where those feelings made sense. But anger came early too. He was expelled from school, and by his mid teens he had spent time in juvenile detention.

[02] THE CELL WHERE IT STARTED
Juvenile detention changed his direction. There he met another young Florida rapper, Stokeley Goulbourne, later known as Ski Mask the Slump God. The two talked about music, freestyled, and dreamed out loud. When they got out, they began recording together. Jahseh chose a strange new name, XXXTentacion, and started uploading songs to SoundCloud. The sound was raw: distorted bass, screamed vocals, and lyrics about depression and isolation. Most people had never heard anything like it. He recorded constantly, sometimes several songs in a single night, on cheap microphones in small bedrooms around Broward County. He did not care if the mix was clean. He cared if the feeling was real.

[03] LOOK AT ME
In late 2015, he released a track called "Look at Me!" It was harsh, chaotic, and impossible to ignore. On SoundCloud it spread from phone to phone, from one teenager to the next. Within two years it had reached the Billboard Hot 100. He was now at the center of a new movement in hip hop, where punk energy met emo honesty. With friends, he formed a collective called Members Only. His fan base grew at a speed few independent artists had ever seen. In 2017, he publicly accused Drake of borrowing the flow of "Look at Me!" for the song "KMT." The feud put his name in headlines across the country, and millions of new listeners went looking for the original.

[04] CHARGES AND CONTROVERSY
His rise was shadowed by serious allegations. In October 2016, he was arrested in Florida and charged with crimes including aggravated battery of a pregnant victim and false imprisonment, involving his former girlfriend. He denied the charges. The case was still pending when he died, so it was never decided in court. For many listeners, these accusations could not be separated from the music. For others, his openness about pain and mental health was what drew them in. Both sides of that debate followed him for the rest of his life.

[05] 17 AND THE STAGE
In 2017, he released the mixtape "Revenge," and then his debut album, "17." It was quiet and dark, almost the opposite of his early songs. One track, "Jocelyn Flores," was written about a friend who had taken her own life. He said he wanted the album to help people who felt alone. On stage, his shows were wild. It debuted at number two on the Billboard 200, an extraordinary result for a young artist whose career had begun on SoundCloud only two years earlier. Crowds pushed toward the front, and the energy often spilled out of control. During his 2017 tour he was attacked on stage in San Diego, and parts of the tour were cancelled.

[06] QUESTION MARK
In March 2018, he released his second album, titled simply with a question mark. It debuted at number one on the Billboard 200. The single "SAD!" became one of the most streamed songs in the country. The album moved between genres: acoustic guitar on "Changes," a slow, heavy bass on "Moonlight," and rock and Latin influences elsewhere. Critics were divided, but listeners were not. They streamed it hundreds of millions of times in a matter of weeks. He spoke more often about wanting to change, about charity work, and about the people who listened to him. He had become one of the most talked about young artists in the world, at only twenty years old.

[07] JUNE 18, 2018
On the afternoon of June 18, 2018, he visited a motorcycle dealership in Deerfield Beach. As he left, his car was blocked by an SUV. Armed men approached, took a bag containing about fifty thousand dollars, and shot him. He was pronounced dead that evening. Fans gathered outside the dealership with candles and flowers. Days later, "SAD!" reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100. Thousands attended a public memorial in Sunrise, Florida. Artists from every corner of hip hop posted tributes. Many of them had never met him, but they had watched a teenager from Florida build something out of nothing.

[08] JUSTICE
Investigators arrested four men. One of them pleaded guilty and testified for the prosecution. In March 2023, a Florida jury convicted the other three of first degree murder and armed robbery. They were sentenced to life in prison without parole. The trial confirmed that this was a robbery. It was not about music. It was about money, and about a young man who was easy to find because he was famous.

[09] LEGACY
Seven months after his death, his son, Gekyume, was born. Posthumous albums followed, including "Skins," which also debuted at number one, and "Bad Vibes Forever" in 2019. Later in 2018, "SAD!" won the American Music Award for Favorite Soul and R and B Song. His mother accepted it on his behalf. In 2022, the documentary "Look at Me" revisited his life, including the voices of those who said he had hurt them. Today his music still draws hundreds of millions of streams. His story remains complicated. He was a gifted artist who spoke openly about depression, and a young man facing serious accusations that were never resolved. He died before he could finish becoming whoever he was going to be.

[10] OUTRO

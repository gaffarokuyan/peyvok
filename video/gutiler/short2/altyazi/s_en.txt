Four thousand two hundred years ago, a people descending from the Zagros mountains toppled the world's first empire.
The Sumerians fearfully called them the Dragon of the Mountains. They were the Gutians.
They brought the Akkadian Empire to its knees and ruled Mesopotamia for a full hundred years.
The Sumerian King List describes them like this: They were their own kings.
Twenty-one kings, a tribal council and local autonomy. Thousands of years later, this order lived on in Kurdish society.
The name Gutium never disappeared. Kurti in Assyrian records, Kardouchoi in Xenophon, Corduene in Roman times, and Kurd today.
According to scholars such as Arshak Safrastian, Godfrey Driver, Ephraim Speiser and Mehrdad Izady, the Gutians are the ancestors of the Kurds.
Hurrians, Mitanni, Lullubi, Kassites, Medes and Corduene: all are the four-thousand-year-old roots of the Kurdish people.

Which is richer, Kurdish or Turkish? First, let's hear from a Turkish poet and writer, Yavuz Bülent Bakiler. In his column for the Türkiye newspaper, Bakiler gives numbers. The dictionary of Mahmud of Kashgar has nine thousand two hundred words. Şemsettin Sami's dictionary has eighteen thousand. The Turkish Language Association's dictionary has eighty thousand. And the pure Turkish dictionary? Only three thousand one hundred and seventy-five words. And Bakiler asks: What can we do with three thousand one hundred and seventy-five words? Now let's look at Kurdish. According to Rudaw, Kurdish ranks eighth on Wiktionary, the world's largest open dictionary. Turkish, Persian and Arabic are behind it. And Kurdish is not a single language, it is a treasury of languages. Kurmanji, Sorani, Kelhuri, Zazaki, Gorani, Laki. So how does a language with no state, no schools, and banned for years, pass the official language of a state and its schools, and write one of the largest dictionaries in the world today?
